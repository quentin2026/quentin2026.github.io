Guanting Liu (刘冠廷)

First-year PhD student at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University.

I work under the supervision of Prof. Shaonan Wang at PolyU and Prof. Chaoming Wang at the Guangdong Institute of Intelligence Science and Technology.

My research interests lie in computational neuroscience, cognitive neuroscience and neurolinguistics.

Research

Task-driven brain modelling

2026 - Present

Large-scale human-connectome-based task-driven brain modelling

Based on an MRI structural connectivity scaffold, I model regional dynamics with firing-rate units and train the network to perform cognitive tasks. I study efficient signal propagation and reliable information routing across the whole brain.

2025 - Present

Multi-levels cognitive computational modelling

Developing biologically constrained models, including leaky integrate-and-fire and Hodgkin-Huxley systems, to investigate working memory and decision-making. Neural-manifold analysis connects computational principles across levels of description.

Cognitive modelling
Whole-brain simulation

2025 - 2026

Whole-brain simulation modelling of zebrafish

Developed a whole-brain zebrafish model with more than 70,000 neurons based on its mesoscale connectome, using whole-brain, single-neuron-resolution calcium signals as training data. The simulated zebrafish reproduces realistic neural activity dynamics, locomotor behavior outputs, visual responses, and neural circuits.

2024 - 2025

Invasive motor brain-computer interface decoding algorithms designing

Optimized classical Kalman-filter algorithms for motor control in brain-computer interfaces, improving computational efficiency, accuracy, and interpretability in motor decoding. I also implemented high-temporal-resolution decoding using spiking neural networks.

Invasive motor BCI
EEG decoding

2021 - 2024

Non-invasive electroencephalogram signal decoding algorithms designing

Combined graph neural networks and convolutional neural networks for EEG decoding in motor imagery and epilepsy detection. I also investigated feature extraction methods for deriving high-signal-to-noise-ratio EEG features.
